Telegram Game Night Planner

A group chat is not a living room. This builds a timed game night for one — rounds, host lines, poll blocks and spoiler-tag reveals, ready to paste into Telegram.

Why a chat game night needs a script at all

In a room, a game runs itself: someone asks, everyone hears it, the next person goes. A Telegram group has none of that. People answer forty minutes apart, two replies land at once and cancel each other out, and the first answer anchors everyone who reads after it. The three fixes below are what this planner bakes into every block it gives you.

1. Use polls when the first answer would spoil the rest

Would You Rather and Most Likely To are ruined the moment one person types their pick — everyone after them is voting on a suggestion, not a choice. Telegram's built-in poll (anonymous by default) collects everyone independently and reveals the split at once. Every poll round here comes with the question and its options already separated, so you paste the question into the poll composer and the options into the option fields.

2. Use spoiler tags for reveals

Wrapping text in ||double pipes|| hides it behind a tap in Telegram. That is what makes Paranoia work in text: the question is public, the name is hidden, and only people who choose to tap see it. Without it the round is just gossip with extra steps.

3. Name a host and pin the rules

Someone has to post the next round. A chat game with no named host dies at round two, every time. Pin the rules message so latecomers can catch up without asking, and say the pace out loud — "one round every ten minutes, answer whenever you see it" — so people who are cooking dinner do not think they have missed it.

What you get here, and what you do not
